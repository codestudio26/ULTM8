Curriculum Video Infrastructure 
 DojoHQ · Product / Engineering Memo
 Hosting and charging for curriculum video
 A recommendation for the video backend behind the Curriculum feature, how to price it for dojo owners, and how it plugs into the grading system that's already built.
 Prepared 25 Aug 2026 · Scope: video infrastructure + monetization + data model
 Recommendation
 Use Cloudflare Stream as the video backend, not a turnkey platform like Uscreen. Uscreen is the wrong category — it's a branded storefront for solo creators, not something you embed inside a product you already built. Its pricing model is worth copying, though: a base fee plus a per-member charge.
 Charge dojos a small base fee plus a per-active-student fee — mirroring how DojoHQ already counts students on the Grading Board, and matching how the underlying hosting cost actually scales.
 01 Two separate decisions
 "Build it like Uscreen" is really two different questions, and they have different answers.
 Where do the bytes live?
 Encoding, storage, and streaming delivery. This is an infrastructure choice — a video API you integrate, not a product you resell as-is.
 How do we charge for it?
 The business model layered on top: what a dojo owner pays DojoHQ to unlock curriculum video for their students.
 Uscreen answers the second question well — it's a mature, tested pricing shape for exactly this kind of video-hosting-as-a-feature business. It answers the first question badly for DojoHQ's situation, because it is the whole product: a branded website, a checkout flow, its own member accounts. There's no clean way to drop "a Uscreen" inside a page that already belongs to the Grading Board. What DojoHQ needs there is a video API — something that takes an upload, encodes it, stores it, and hands back a player and a bill by the minute.

 Why Cloudflare Stream
 Curriculum video for a martial arts gym is low-traffic, high-value content — a few dozen students rewatching technique breakdowns before grading, not a viral audience. At that profile, the difference between Cloudflare's and Bunny's raw bill is a few dollars a month per dojo; it doesn't move the decision. What does: Cloudflare ships signed playback URLs and time-limited tokens natively, so "only this dojo's active students can watch this video" is a few lines of code, not a project. Two pricing lines instead of four make it trivial to build a clean markup on top for the add-on tier below. And it's already a mature, boring, reliable platform — boring is good for something billing customers depend on.
 Worth revisiting Bunny.net once a handful of large dojo chains push delivery volume into the hundreds of TB/month — that's the point its per-GB pricing pulls meaningfully ahead. Worth revisiting Mux if the analytics (completion rates, engagement by video) become a selling point of Curriculum itself rather than a nice-to-have.
 03 Charging dojo owners
 The instinct to charge "for the display of the video" is right, but billing a dojo owner directly in GB or minutes would be a bad experience — nobody running a gym thinks in bandwidth. Uscreen's model translates well here: a modest base fee that unlocks the feature and covers typical storage, plus a small per-member charge that scales with the thing that actually drives DojoHQ's cost — how many students are watching. DojoHQ already counts active students for the Grading Board, so this reuses data that exists rather than inventing a new metric.
 Worked example — a mid-size dojo
 Active students 80 
 Avg. curriculum viewing 20 min / student / mo 
 Total delivered 1,600 min / mo 
 Delivery cost (Cloudflare, $1/1,000 min) $1.60 
 Curriculum library (10 hrs, 1080p) 600 min stored 
 Storage cost ($5 / 1,000 min block) $3.00 
 DojoHQ's raw hosting cost ≈ $4.60 / mo 
 At roughly $5/month in raw cost for a fairly active dojo, there's wide room to price a Curriculum add-on well above cost and still land far below what Uscreen or Vimeo OTT would charge for the same gym to run standalone. Two shapes worth prototyping:
 Flat add-on: a fixed monthly fee (e.g. in the $19–39 range) that unlocks Curriculum for the whole gym, storage included up to a generous cap. Simple to sell, simple to bill, and the margin absorbs normal usage swings.
 Base + per-student: a lower base fee plus a small per-active-student charge (well under $1/student), closer to Uscreen's Growth tier shape. Scales fairly for a 20-student garage dojo versus a 300-student chain, and ties the price directly to the value delivered.
 Either is comfortably above the ~90% gross margin video-hosting businesses typically run at this usage profile. The per-student shape is the better long-term fit given DojoHQ's existing data model, but the flat add-on is the faster thing to ship and test pricing sensitivity with first.
 04 How it plugs into grading
 The part that matters most for this app specifically: curriculum content shouldn't live as a separate, disconnected video library. It should attach directly to the Skills and Ranks that already exist in DB.skills and each style's rank ladder, so a video is something a student watches because it's required for their next grade, not something they browse to on their own.

 Shape of the data model
 Category
 A curriculum section — likely one per Style to start (e.g. "IBJJF Adult & Kid"), matching the structure Styles & Ranks already uses.
 Lesson
 Title, text/description, one video (playback id from Cloudflare), and an optional link to one or more skillId s or a specific rank.
 With that link in place, two places in the existing app get richer for free: the Skill Library can show a "has video" badge and an inline player on a skill's detail, and the Grade modal's skills-required warning can carry a direct "watch the technique" link instead of just naming the skill.
 05 Suggested next step
 1 Mock up the Curriculum tab and a Lesson card in the same prototype file, reusing the app's existing card/modal patterns — no real video yet, just the shape and the skill-link UI, shown as a JPG before anything gets wired up.
 2 Once the UI is approved, wire a single real upload → Cloudflare Stream → playback round trip for one test video, to confirm signed-URL gating actually behaves the way this memo assumes.
 3 Decide flat-add-on vs. base-plus-per-student pricing by sketching both as an actual settings-page mockup and picking the one that's easier to explain to a dojo owner in one sentence.
 Open question
 This assumes each dojo uploads and owns its own curriculum footage. If DojoHQ ever wants to sell a pre-built curriculum library (professionally filmed, licensed across dojos), that's a different content and rights model layered on the same video backend — worth flagging now, not worth designing until this first version ships.
